# Calibration studies — the recipes behind the calibration figures

Companion to `TRACKING_STUDIES.md`, for the calibration half of the paper. Everything here is
read off `utils/calibration.py`, `utils/calib_run.py` and the 2D pipeline, or is a measurement
from the 2026-07/08 revision campaign. Measurements are marked **[measured]**; statements that
are plausible but not established are marked **[?]** and must not be stated as fact.

Ported 2026-08-12 from the revision campaign's recipe and handover notes, which are not part of
this repository.

---

## 1. What the calibration is

A **closure demonstration**. Truth data are generated by the same differentiable simulator that
is then fitted, from a start displaced from truth, and the fit recovers the parameters.

**Estimand — 19 parameters:** `{scatter_length, absorption_length, QE}` independently at each of
5 wavelengths (337, 375, 405, 445, 473 nm — the SK water-calibration laser lines,
arXiv:1307.0162), plus 4 shared reflection parameters, fitted as `[log R_w, logit f_w, log R_s,
logit f_s]`. The optics are per-wavelength and independent: no spectral model ties them together.
The reflection parameters are shared across all wavelengths (`scalar_mix` carries no wavelength
dependence).

**Nuisance:** `NS = 10764` per-PMT gains, profiled in closed form each step under a
`mean(log k) = 0` gauge. They are not among the 19.

**Data:** 8 sources (1 collimated laser from the top + 7 isotropic at centre and ±10 m on each
axis) × 5 wavelengths = 40 configurations × 10764 PMTs. **Charge only; no timing.**

**Frozen:** Mie scattering (`mie_scatter_length = 1e6`, `g = 0.9`). The fitted `scatter_length`
is therefore Rayleigh-only — the asymmetric channel `alpha_asym`, 1.4–6.3% of the scattering
rate depending on wavelength, is **dropped, not folded in**.

## 2. The estimator

### It is a quasi-score / GEE, not a least-squares fit

With residual `r = (k·M − Q)/√Q` and `J = ∂r/∂θ`, the update solves

```
Jᵀr  =  Dᵀ diag(1/Q) (k·M − Q)  =  0        D = ∂(k·M)/∂θ
```

which is exactly the GEE / quasi-score `U(θ) = Dᵀ V⁻¹ (y − μ(θ)) = 0` with working covariance
`V = diag(Q)`, the Poisson working variance (Liang & Zeger 1986). `Jᵀr` is not a modelling
choice: for `L = ½Σrᵢ²` it *is* the gradient exactly, and `JᵀJ` is the Gauss-Newton curvature
obtained free from the same Jacobian.

### Why the Neyman χ² is forced, not preferred

The forward model `M` is a stochastic Monte-Carlo estimate **redrawn every step**. Any loss
nonlinear in `M` therefore has a biased gradient (`E[f(M)] ≠ f(E[M])`), and that bias displaces
the fixed point permanently — no amount of iteration recovers from it.

| candidate | form | linear in M? |
|---|---|---|
| Poisson NLL | `Σ[μ − q log μ]` | no (`log M`) |
| sqrt / variance-stabilised | `√(kM) − √Q` | no (`√M`) |
| Pearson χ² | `(kM−Q)/√(kM)` | no (`M` in the weight) |
| **Neyman χ²** | `(kM−Q)/√Q` | **yes** — the weight is data-only |

Neyman is the only one of the four that survives.

### The weight is mis-specified, and that is tolerable

**[measured]** `Var/μ` per configuration at truth, 16 draws: laser 4.5–10.1, isotropic 0.47–2.25.
A 22× spread, and 14 of 40 configurations are *under*-dispersed. The `1/Q` weight assumes 1
uniformly, so the laser is over-weighted 5.2× relative to the isotropic sources and efficiency is
**51.7%** (1.93× variance inflation, 1.39× on σ).

GEE theory is what licenses this: the estimator is consistent for *any* working covariance
provided the mean model is correct; efficiency alone requires the true covariance. The
mis-specification costs variance, not correctness.

## 3. Three evaluation separations, each load-bearing

1. **The residual uses a fresh draw every step**, so the estimator targets the *expected*
   objective rather than the minimum of one noisy realisation. **[measured]** freezing the
   forward key (sample-average approximation) is **5.0× worse** (6.50% vs 1.29% mean
   worst-parameter error).

2. **The Jacobian uses an independent key stream.** The single most important evaluation choice.
   With shared draws `E[Jᵀr] = E[J]ᵀE[r] + Cov(J,r)`; **[measured]** paired test at truth, N=96
   draws, `Cov(J,r)` reaches **137σ** and is **15× the per-draw gradient noise**. It is free to
   remove — per-draw noise 9376 shared vs 9180 independent, ratio 0.979, **no variance penalty**.
   This is also why `jax.grad` of a scalar loss cannot be used: AD differentiates one realisation,
   so it shares the draw by construction.

   ⚠️ **The independence is incomplete.** `_jbody` receives `lk = log(Qsum/Msum)` computed from
   the *residual's* draw and multiplies the model by `exp(lk)` inside the Jacobian, so `J` and `r`
   share the random variable `lk` even though their photon streams are disjoint. It vanishes
   exactly under `GAINS=fixed`. Magnitude **[?] unmeasured** — do not claim it is small.

3. **The readout is a Polyak tail average.** The trajectory never settles; it wanders on the MC
   noise floor. **[measured]** on `long600`, 3 seeds, mean worst-parameter error by window:
   W=1 (final iterate) 3.80%, W=50 3.43%, **W=200 2.87%**, W=300 3.20%. The optimum is near
   W≈200.

**The metric is a preconditioner only.** `JᵀJ` cannot move the fixed point, so its known noise
inflation costs convergence *rate*, not accuracy — **[measured]** confirmed by substituting the
true Poisson Fisher as the metric.

## 4. Figure B — joint 19-parameter convergence

`fig_calib_convergence.py` → `utils/calib_run.py` (per seed, in-process) → `utils/calib_plots.convergence`.

This ran through the campaign's engine as a subprocess per seed until the fit moved into
`lucid.fitting`. That engine is not in this repository: the library path was verified against it
when the fit moved, and that check is not shipped.

Recipe: `utils/calibration.py:CALIB_RECIPE`, which is the knob set of the campaign's
`steps600.sh` driver (not in this repository). Every run records its own knobs into its `.npz` — including the estimator it ran
(`loss`, `gauge`, `metric`, `hcorr`, `solver`) and not only its cost knobs — so a saved run is
self-describing and can be checked against the table without trusting the driver.

### Why 600 iterations — the two DIFFUSE components, and nothing else

**[measured]** on `long600` (3 seeds), seed-to-seed spread of the Polyak readout, in percentage
points of truth:

| iteration | 100 | 200 | 300 | 400 | 500 | 600 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| optics — max over the 15 `{L_s, L_a, QE}` | 2.81 | 0.89 | 0.57 | **0.19** | 0.18 | 0.34 |
| specular — max over `Rw_spec`, `Rs_spec` | 1.08 | 0.48 | 0.35 | **0.11** | 0.11 | 0.09 |
| **diffuse — max over `Rw_diff`, `Rs_diff`** | **27.75** | **10.92** | **5.92** | **3.01** | **1.18** | **1.43** |

Optics and specular are at or below ~0.2 pp by iteration 400 and flat from ~300. The diffuse pair
is still at 3.0 pp at 400 and only reaches ~1.2–1.4 pp by 500–600. **The diffuse components are
slow, not unconstrained.** The 500→600 uptick is within the noise of a 3-seed spread estimate;
do not read it as divergence.

### The optimizer

**[measured]** `cond(F) ≈ 6800` on the 19-parameter matrix; on the 2D slice Gauss-Newton
converged in 19–25 iterations against gradient descent's 152–278. A first-order method is not
viable. Step: `A = H + LAM·diag(H) + MU·median(diag H)·I`, then `solve(A, g)`, clipped per
component at `STEP_MAX`.

- **[measured]** `LAM` is **inert** — `LAM=0` is indistinguishable from baseline.
- **[measured]** `MU` is **load-bearing** — `MU=0.3` is catastrophic (over-damps the soft
  reflection directions; `Rs_diff` reaches +11.2% with a sign flip), `MU=0.03` is mixed.
- **[measured]** `STEP_MAX=0.5` binds only in the first ~10 iterations and never after 50.

### Convergence criterion

**[measured]** χ² **cannot certify convergence**: it plateaus by iteration ~20 while the
reflection error runs from 200% to 16%. The objective is dominated by the stiff optics
directions; the soft reflection directions are 10³–10⁴× less visible in it. The smoothed step
norm `‖Δθ‖` does work — it falls 17× monotonically (0.181 → 0.011 over 200 steps, smoothed over
20) and references no truth. **This is the criterion to quote.** Nothing currently computes it.

## 5. Figure A — 2D loss geometry

`fig_calib_loss_geometry.py` → `compute_2d_neyman.py` (×N shards) → `combine_2d.py` →
`traj_2d_neyman.py` → `utils/calib_plots.loss_geometry`.

A 51×51 grid over `(λ_s, λ_a)` at 405 nm, all other parameters pinned at truth, **one collimated
laser** from the top of the tank, `INTEN=1e8`, `SK_like`. Both panels show the same `log₁₀ L`
surface and differ only in the vector field and the overlaid paths: left `−∇L`, right
`−F⁻¹∇L` with `F` the per-point Fisher. **[measured]** GD takes 278 and 152 iterations from the
two starts; GN takes 25 and 19.

Recipe: `utils/calibration.py:LANDSCAPE_RECIPE`. Stage 3 **must** carry the same `K` / `N_PH` /
`NK_*` as stage 1, which is why both read them from that one table rather than from flags.

Two operational requirements, both learned the hard way:

- `XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION=0.95` is **required**. JAX's 0.75 default leaves ~8.4 GiB of an
  11.26 GiB card against a 9.51 GiB peak, and every shard OOMs. Set in `LANDSCAPE_ENV`.
- **A shard-count and freshness check must run before combining.** Without it, a run in which
  every shard OOMs leaves the previous landscape `.npz` in place and the figure silently
  re-renders stale data. `fig_calib_loss_geometry.py` enforces both and refuses to combine.

**[measured]** `K=8` vs `K=12` costs 0.0015% on the loss at truth and buys 1.54× the rays; the MC
noise floor of the surface falls as `1/N_PH`, so rays are what make this figure smoother. `INTEN`
is not a lever here — the engine is expected-value, so intensity rescales the surface and changes
nothing.

## 6. Caveats that must appear in the text

1. **Closure / inverse crime.** Truth and forward are the same simulator. Quoted accuracies
   characterise the estimator, not end-to-end accuracy against nature.
2. **Emission is shared.** **[measured]** the isotropic sources use a Fibonacci lattice that
   *ignores its PRNG key* (bit-identical across keys). At K=8 the direct term is 51.4% of the
   total charge, so a "different seed" changes transport only. The lattice is a *good* quadrature
   — 1.46% per-PMT deviation from ideal isotropic at `N_PH=1e6`, 6× better than random sampling
   at equal N — but the closure does not test emission. `TRUTH_RANDOM_ISO=1` (via
   `isotropic_source_random`) is the control arm that breaks this.
3. **Charge only.** No timing. Reflection is consequently the weakest-determined block.
4. **Source diversity is ~3 configurations, not 7.** **[measured]** detector symmetry makes
   ±x/±y/±z pairs differ by <0.5%; the distinct patterns are {laser, centre, radial, axial}.
5. **Mie is dropped**, not folded in (§1).
6. **Reflection is λ-flat while optics are λ-resolved.** Any true λ-dependence in reflectivity has
   only the free per-λ optics to be absorbed into.
7. **The reporting basis amplifies.** **[measured]** we fit `(log R, logit f)` but report `R·f`
   and `R·(1−f)`. Since `d log(1−f)/d log f = −f/(1−f) = −9` at `f_s = 0.90`, the fitted
   parameters recover to ≤0.4% while `Rs_diff` reads −2.6%. **Quote `(R, f)`, or state the lever.**
8. **The residual bias is photon-statistics-limited.** **[measured]** with gains held fixed,
   `NB_RES` 1→4 moves `Rs_diff` from −1.85% to +0.01%. Not an estimator pathology.
9. **`INTEN` is not a photon budget.** **[measured]** it is exactly multiplicative
   (`q(1e8)/q(1e6) = 100.00000`) and the Gauss-Newton step is provably invariant to it. It sets
   the charge scale and the quoted CRB (`σ ∝ 1/√INTEN`) and has **zero** effect on the fit.

## 7. Known gaps — do not state these as fact

- The `lk` channel in the J/r independence (§3.2) — second order, size unknown.
- Whether a per-config weight correction improves the reflection recovery. The efficiency loss is
  measured; the effect on the answer is not.
- The behind-sensor deposit (+8.4% charge at `R_s=0.25`) and `mcpr=4` (`R_s` response ~5% from
  converged). Both cancel under closure but would matter for any real-data claim. The fix exists
  on `fix/behind-sensor-deposit` and is **not** in this baseline.
- `HCORR` has never been run in 137 archived runs; the metric is knowingly noise-inflated
  (`N/H = 1.4` on the score directions). Affects rate, not the fixed point.
- The laser fibre sits 0.15 m **inside** the top-cap PMT (`TOP = H/2 − 0.1 = 18.0 m`, PMT centre
  18.1 m, radius 0.25 m). **[measured]** that PMT takes ~1% of the laser charge with ~7× the `L_s`
  leverage of the whole detector.
- **The CRB appendix describes code that has never run.** All 137 archived `crb_part_*.npz` have
  `crbcov` identically zero; `COMPUTE_CRB=1` appears nowhere in the campaign drivers. The
  Schur/gauge algebra is correct; it has simply never been executed. Either run it or remove the
  appendix claims.

## 8. Divergences between the manuscript and this code

Recorded so they are not rediscovered. From the 2026-08-03 audit.

- **The submitted convergence figure is not this recipe.** It came from `conv_recipe2`
  (`steps=400, polyak=0`, legacy eigen-floored solver, `JKEY_SEED` unset), verified from the
  saved `.npz`. The reported value is therefore the **final iterate**, not a tail average — 32%
  worse than W=200 and with a much wider seed spread. `CALIB_RECIPE` here is the corrected
  recipe, and `long600` is the run that matches it.
- **Two tex files disagreed about the loss** — `revised_sections.tex` claimed the √-residual
  objective, `main_revised.tex` the Neyman χ². Production sets `LOSS=neyman`, so `main_revised`
  is right; the surrounding derivation in `revised_sections.tex` appeals to the information
  equality, which the Neyman χ² does not satisfy, so it needs rewriting rather than patching.
